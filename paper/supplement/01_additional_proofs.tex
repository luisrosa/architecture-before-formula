\section{Additional proofs and quantitative results}\label{supp:proofs}

The main article derives the exact receiver-interface, attention, aggregation, feedforward, and whole-block results. This section adds quantitative statements that are useful when one of the exact commitments is only approximately realized.

\subsection{Entropy-regularized receiver-row anchoring}

The main article derives normalized positive allocation from ratio preservation and unit receiver mass. The same row anchor is the unique solution of a receiver-local regularized optimization problem.

\begin{proposition}[Entropy-regularized row anchor]\label{prop:supp-entropy-anchor}
Let $S$ be finite, let $\Gamma_i>0$, let $s_i\in\R$, and let $\tau>0$. With the convention $0\log 0=0$, the problem
\[
\max_{p\in\Delta(S)}
\left\{
\sum_{i\in S}p_is_i
-\tau\sum_{i\in S}p_i\log\frac{p_i}{\Gamma_i}
\right\}
\]
has the unique solution
\[
\boxed{
p_i
=
\frac{\Gamma_i e^{s_i/\tau}}
{\sum_{j\in S}\Gamma_j e^{s_j/\tau}}.
}
\]
A hard mask is implemented by replacing $S$ with the nonempty admissible support.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The objective is continuous on the compact simplex and therefore attains a maximum. Its entropy term is strictly concave on the relative interior, while the linear score term does not change concavity. Moreover, the one-sided derivative of $-p_i\log p_i$ diverges to $+\infty$ as $p_i\downarrow0$, so the maximizer has positive coordinates on the declared support.

Introduce a multiplier $\lambda$ for $\sum_i p_i=1$. The first-order condition is
\[
s_i
-\tau\left(\log\frac{p_i}{\Gamma_i}+1\right)
-\lambda
=0.
\]
Hence
\[
p_i
=
\Gamma_i
\exp\!\left(\frac{s_i-\lambda-\tau}{\tau}\right)
=
c\Gamma_i e^{s_i/\tau}
\]
for a common positive constant $c$. Normalization gives
\[
c^{-1}=\sum_j\Gamma_j e^{s_j/\tau}.
\]
Strict concavity gives uniqueness.
\end{proof}

The result supplies a second interpretation of row-softmax: it is not merely a normalization convention but the optimizer of a declared score-versus-relative-entropy tradeoff. That interpretation still depends on choosing a receiver-local simplex, a prior $\Gamma$, and an entropy regularizer. Other anchors correspond to different feasible sets or regularizers.

\subsection{Softmax stability}

For $z\in\R^n$ and temperature $\tau>0$, write
\[
\sigma_\tau(z)_i
:=
\frac{e^{z_i/\tau}}{\sum_j e^{z_j/\tau}}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Softmax Lipschitz bound]\label{lem:supp-softmax-lipschitz}
For all $z,z'\in\R^n$,
\[
\boxed{
\norm{\sigma_\tau(z)-\sigma_\tau(z')}_1
\le
\frac{2}{\tau}\norm{z-z'}_\infty.
}
\]
The same bound holds when both rows use the same fixed nonempty mask, after restricting to the admissible coordinates.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $p=\sigma_\tau(z)$. The Jacobian is
\[
D\sigma_\tau(z)
=
\tau^{-1}\left(\mathrm{Diag}(p)-pp^\top\right).
\]
For any $u$ with $\norm{u}_\infty\le1$, set $\mu=p^\top u$. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\norm{\left(\mathrm{Diag}(p)-pp^\top\right)u}_1
&=
\sum_i p_i\abs{u_i-\mu}\\
&\le
2\sum_i p_i
=2,
\end{aligned}
\]
because $u_i,\mu\in[-1,1]$. Thus the operator norm from $\ell_\infty$ to $\ell_1$ is at most $2/\tau$. Integrating the Jacobian along the segment $z_t=z'+t(z-z')$ gives
\[
\sigma_\tau(z)-\sigma_\tau(z')
=
\int_0^1D\sigma_\tau(z_t)(z-z')\,dt,
\]
and the stated bound follows.
\end{proof}

The bound makes temperature an explicit stability commitment. Smaller $\tau$ permits sharper allocations but amplifies score perturbations in this worst-case estimate.

\subsection{Rank-restricted score approximation}

Let carrier states satisfy $\norm{h_i}\le R$. Consider a bilinear score
\[
s_{ij}=h_i^\top Mh_j
\]
and a rank-$d$ approximation $M_d$.

\begin{proposition}[Rank-truncated attention error]\label{prop:supp-rank-error}
Let $p_i$ and $\widehat p_i$ be the receiver-row softmax distributions generated by $M$ and $M_d$ at temperature $\tau$ on the same fixed nonempty support. Then
\[
\boxed{
\norm{p_i-\widehat p_i}_1
\le
\frac{2R^2}{\tau}
\norm{M-M_d}_{\mathrm{op}}.
}
\]
If $\norm{Vh_j}\le B$ and $O$ is a linear output map, then
\[
\boxed{
\norm{
O\sum_j(p_{ij}-\widehat p_{ij})Vh_j
}
\le
\frac{2\norm{O}_{\mathrm{op}}BR^2}{\tau}
\norm{M-M_d}_{\mathrm{op}}.
}
\]
For a best rank-$d$ approximation in operator norm,
\[
\norm{M-M_d}_{\mathrm{op}}=\sigma_{d+1}(M).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For every admissible pair,
\[
\abs{h_i^\top(M-M_d)h_j}
\le
\norm{h_i}\norm{M-M_d}_{\mathrm{op}}\norm{h_j}
\le
R^2\norm{M-M_d}_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]
Therefore the row-logit perturbation has $\ell_\infty$ norm at most $R^2\norm{M-M_d}_{\mathrm{op}}$. Apply \Cref{lem:supp-softmax-lipschitz}.

For the output,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\norm{
O\sum_j(p_{ij}-\widehat p_{ij})Vh_j
}
&\le
\norm{O}_{\mathrm{op}}
\sum_j\abs{p_{ij}-\widehat p_{ij}}\norm{Vh_j}\\
&\le
\norm{O}_{\mathrm{op}}B\norm{p_i-\widehat p_i}_1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Substitute the first bound. The final equality is the Eckart--Young characterization of the best rank-$d$ operator-norm approximation.
\end{proof}

A rank budget is therefore a quantitative commitment distinct from the structural claim that the score family is shared and separable. The latter supplies common query--key coordinates; the former controls the approximation defect of a restricted coordinate dimension.

\subsection{A routed--local approximation ledger}

The main article proves an exact decomposition for an instantiated canonical block. A separate question asks when a general target update can be approximated by one routed branch followed by one local branch.

Let $K\subseteq V^S$ be compact, let $\widetilde K\subseteq V^S$ be a compact ambient set, and define
\[
\Phi_\varepsilon(H)=H+\varepsilon\Delta(H).
\]
Assume that on $K$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:supp-structural-decomposition}
\Delta(H)
=
A(H)+L(H)+R_{\mathrm{mix}}(H),
\end{equation}
where $A$ is the selected routed contribution, $L$ the selected local contribution, and $R_{\mathrm{mix}}$ the residual structural defect. Let $\widehat A$ and $\widehat L$ be realizations satisfying
\[
K\cup
\{H+\varepsilon\widehat A(H):H\in K\}
\subseteq\widetilde K.
\]
Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
\rho_\infty
&:=
\sup_{H\in K}\norm{A(H)-\widehat A(H)},\\
m_\infty
&:=
\sup_{H\in K}\norm{R_{\mathrm{mix}}(H)},\\
\delta_\infty
&:=
\sup_{H\in\widetilde K}\norm{L(H)-\widehat L(H)},\\
\widehat a
&:=
\sup_{H\in K}\norm{\widehat A(H)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Assume $L$ is $\Lambda$-Lipschitz on $\widetilde K$.

\begin{theorem}[Generic staged approximation]\label{thm:supp-staged-approximation}
The sequential approximation
\[
H^{(1)}=H+\varepsilon\widehat A(H),
\qquad
T_\varepsilon(H)
=
H^{(1)}+\varepsilon\widehat L(H^{(1)})
\]
satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{H\in K}
\norm{\Phi_\varepsilon(H)-T_\varepsilon(H)}
\le
\varepsilon
\left(
 m_\infty+\rho_\infty+\delta_\infty
\right)
+
\varepsilon^2\Lambda\widehat a.
}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $H\in K$, use \eqref{eq:supp-structural-decomposition} to write
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_\varepsilon(H)-T_\varepsilon(H)
&=
\varepsilon(A(H)-\widehat A(H))
+
\varepsilon R_{\mathrm{mix}}(H)\\
&\quad+
\varepsilon
\left[
L(H)-\widehat L(H+\varepsilon\widehat A(H))
\right].
\end{aligned}
\]
Insert and subtract $L(H+\varepsilon\widehat A(H))$. The first two terms are bounded by $\varepsilon(\rho_\infty+m_\infty)$. The local realization defect contributes at most $\varepsilon\delta_\infty$. Lipschitz continuity gives
\[
\varepsilon
\norm{L(H)-L(H+\varepsilon\widehat A(H))}
\le
\varepsilon^2\Lambda\norm{\widehat A(H)}
\le
\varepsilon^2\Lambda\widehat a.
\]
Take the supremum over $K$.
\end{proof}

The terms have different meanings:
\begin{itemize}
\item $m_\infty$ measures failure of the selected routed--local structural hypothesis;
\item $\rho_\infty$ measures finite routed-realization error;
\item $\delta_\infty$ measures finite local-realization error;
\item $\varepsilon^2\Lambda\widehat a$ is the first schedule-composition correction.
\end{itemize}
No single term should be reported as ``the Transformer approximation error'' without declaring its source.

\subsection{Controlled residual continuation}

Residual addition supplies a marked identity path, but it does not unconditionally preserve every distinction after the branch is added.

\begin{lemma}[Injective controlled residual]\label{lem:supp-residual-injective}
Let $C\subseteq\R^d$, let $\phi:C\to\R^p$ be $L_\phi$-Lipschitz, and let $J:\R^p\to\R^d$ be linear. Define
\[
\Psi_\varepsilon(z)
=
z+\varepsilon J\phi(z).
\]
If
\[
\varepsilon\norm{J}_{\mathrm{op}}L_\phi<1,
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
\norm{\Psi_\varepsilon(z)-\Psi_\varepsilon(z')}
\ge
\left(1-\varepsilon\norm{J}_{\mathrm{op}}L_\phi\right)
\norm{z-z'}.
}
\]
Hence $\Psi_\varepsilon$ is injective on $C$ and its inverse on $\Psi_\varepsilon(C)$ is Lipschitz with constant at most
\[
\left(1-\varepsilon\norm{J}_{\mathrm{op}}L_\phi\right)^{-1}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By the reverse triangle inequality,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\norm{\Psi_\varepsilon(z)-\Psi_\varepsilon(z')}
&\ge
\norm{z-z'}
-
\varepsilon\norm{J}_{\mathrm{op}}
\norm{\phi(z)-\phi(z')}\\
&\ge
\left(1-\varepsilon\norm{J}_{\mathrm{op}}L_\phi\right)
\norm{z-z'}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The positive lower factor gives injectivity and the inverse Lipschitz estimate.
\end{proof}

Without such a condition, an additive residual map can be noninjective. Identity continuation is a marked architectural path, not an unconditional theorem that the total successor map preserves all information.

\subsection{Finite-support interpolation}

\begin{proposition}[Finite represented-state interpolation]\label{prop:supp-finite-interpolation}
Let $z_1,\ldots,z_M\in\R^d$ be distinct and let $y_1,\ldots,y_M\in\R^p$. There exists a finite-width two-layer ReLU network $f$ such that
\[
f(z_m)=y_m
\]
for every $m$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose $a\in\R^d$ outside the finite union of hyperplanes
\[
\bigcup_{m\neq n}\{a:a^\top(z_m-z_n)=0\}.
\]
Then the scalars $t_m=a^\top z_m$ are pairwise distinct. Order them and construct a vector-valued continuous piecewise-linear map $g:\R\to\R^p$ satisfying $g(t_m)=y_m$. Every scalar continuous piecewise-linear function on the line is an affine function plus a finite sum of ReLU hinges; apply this coordinatewise to $g$. Then
\[
f(z):=g(a^\top z)
\]
is a finite-width two-layer ReLU network with the required interpolation values.
\end{proof}

Finite-sample memorization therefore does not identify architecture. The relevant question is which marked interfaces, sharing laws, and continuation structure generated those values and how the construction behaves beyond the finite support.
